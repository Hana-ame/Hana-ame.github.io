Clifton Suspension Bridge

Overview

The Clifton Suspension Bridge is a distinctive and iconic landmark spanning the Avon Gorge and the River Avon. It connects the Bristol suburb of Clifton to Leigh Woods in North Somerset, England.

Historical Background

Designed by the celebrated Victorian engineer Isambard Kingdom Brunel, the bridge marks a significant point in British engineering history. Although Brunel designed the bridge in the early 1830s, severe funding issues delayed the construction. The bridge was ultimately completed and opened in 1864, five years after Brunel's death, serving as a memorial to his incredible life's work.

Key Facts

  • Location: Bristol, England
  • Designer: Isambard Kingdom Brunel (completed by William Henry Barlow and John Hawkshaw)
  • Opened: December 8, 1864
  • Total Length: 1,352 feet (412 meters)
  • Clearance Below: 245 feet (75 meters) above high water level
  • Listed Status: Grade I Listed Building
